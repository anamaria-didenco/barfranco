Our story

The Italy you didn’t want to leave.

Let me be Franco with you: I’m not Italian, and the soul of this place isn’t either.

Everything I know about hospitality, I learned in Moldova, at my grandmother’s table. It was never complicated:

  • No one’s glass is ever empty.
  • Everyone is fed.
  • Every person through the door is a long-lost friend we’ve been waiting to see.

That isn’t a service standard we trained into the staff. It’s just how you treat people where I’m from.

And here’s the part people don’t expect. My grandmother isn’t rich. Moldova isn’t a rich country. But you’d never know it at her table. Whatever there is, all of it comes out for you. Abundance was never about how much you had; it was about how much you gave. You leave feeling like the richest person in the room.

The table at the centre of Bar Franco is my grandmother’s, carried all the way from Moldova. The name above the door is my mother’s: the Franco script is her own hand, her signature, kept. Three generations, one country, one way of treating people. I just gave it an Italian accent.

Because I did fall for Italy. The long lunches, the piano piano, the slowly-slowly, the way a table there can hold you for hours. I refused to come home and leave all of it behind. So I didn’t. I built the world I didn’t want to leave, and I furnished it with everything my family had already taught me.

Franco means frank. Honest. It’s the name, and it’s the rule. No costume, no cliché, no pretending to be something we’re not. Just pasta made by hand, a Negroni poured properly, and a room that genuinely wants you in it.

Two levels. One world.

Downstairs, the Negroni and Spritz bar, where the evening starts slow.

Upstairs, the open kitchen, where we cook like someone’s coming home.

You’ll find us tucked off Cashel Street, the kind of door you have to know. Some places want to be seen. Bar Franco wants to be felt.

Come as you are. Leave slower than you arrived.

Ti aspettiamo. We’re waiting for you. 🖤

— Ana-Maria
